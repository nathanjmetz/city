# Real-life details for Neighbors (Grant County, Indiana)

**Purpose.** This is a long menu of small, true-to-life details that Neighbors can grow from its own rules, so the owner finds them while playing instead of placing them by hand. Each idea is a few stored facts plus a real-world rule, drawn in more or less detail depending on how far it is from the camera.

Written 2026-10-25, from the owner's request that day: a list "far longer than I would even check by hand," built a piece at a time, with him reporting only what "doesn't look realistic."

## How to read the lines

Each line is one idea:

- **Short name**: what the player notices.
- *Rule:* what causes it in real life. Numbers are rough and from general knowledge of east-central Indiana. Check any number before it becomes a hard rule, and tell the owner when one is a guess.
- *Facts:* the few things the game would store. Everything else is worked out from these, from the date, and from the town's own life.
- *Where:* browser, desktop, or both. "Desktop" means the full-detail close-up version. Browser versions are usually the lighter or numbers-only version.
- *Cost:* low, medium, or high effort and computer load. Low is often a few lines on top of facts the game already has.

Detail by distance applies to every idea: full detail near the camera, a lighter look farther out, and just numbers beyond that. Many ideas share the same facts (the date, the weather, a person's income, a road's age), so building a few shared facts well unlocks dozens of lines below.

Shared facts used all over: the date and time; today's weather and the last 7 days of it; sunrise and sunset for 40.5 degrees north; each person's age, job, income, tastes; each building's age, owner income, and last repair date; each road's age, last repair date, and traffic count; each vehicle's age, owner income, and trip history.

## 1. The ground and wear

- **Desire paths in the grass**: A bare dirt line appears across a lawn or park where people cut the corner. *Rule:* grass dies under repeated foot traffic; roughly a few hundred passes a month wears turf to soil, and it takes a season to grow back. *Facts:* a wear number per small patch of ground, bumped each time someone steps there, and healed by the season. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Worn spot under the swings**: The dirt under swing sets and at the bottom of slides is bare and hollowed. *Rule:* kids' feet drag the same spot every day, and rain runs the loose dirt away. *Facts:* wear number, play equipment position. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Bus-stop dirt circle**: Kids waiting for the school bus leave a trampled, muddy ring at the corner. *Rule:* a crowd of kids stands in one place twice a day, 180 school days a year. *Facts:* wear per patch, bus stop location. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Path to the mailbox**: A faint path in the lawn runs from the porch to the curb or road mailbox. *Rule:* one trip a day by one person is enough to show in turf over a year. *Facts:* house door, mailbox position, wear number. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Dog-run strip along the fence**: A bare stripe runs along a fenced yard where a dog paces. *Rule:* dogs patrol a fence line again and again, and urine burns the grass. *Facts:* has a dog, fence line, wear number. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Brown dog-pee spots**: Round yellow-brown patches dot lawns where a dog lives. *Rule:* nitrogen in dog urine burns grass, usually with a green ring around the dead spot. *Facts:* has a dog, lawn patch. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Ruts at the gate**: Muddy twin ruts form at a field gate and farm lane in wet weather. *Rule:* heavy tractors and trucks press wet clay soil down and it stays deformed when it dries. *Facts:* lane position, vehicle weight count, soil wetness. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Tire ruts on the lawn corner**: Cars that cut a driveway or street corner leave tire tracks in the grass. *Rule:* wide turns by trucks and trailers roll over the corner turf again and again. *Facts:* corner geometry, pass counts. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Gravel driveway ruts and potholes**: Gravel drives show two wheel tracks and low spots that pool water. *Rule:* gravel is pushed aside by tires and washes out in heavy rain; owners add a load every few years. *Facts:* drive surface, passes, last gravel date. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Gravel kicked onto the road**: Loose stones spill out where a gravel drive meets the pavement. *Rule:* tires carry gravel up onto the edge of the pavement at every entrance. *Facts:* drive surface type, traffic. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Mud tracked out from fields**: Brown mud trails stretch along county roads from field entrances in spring and fall. *Rule:* tires and tractors carry wet soil onto pavement until rain washes it off. *Facts:* field work today, soil wetness, road. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Dust clouds on gravel roads**: Cars on gravel roads trail a long dust cloud in dry weather, and none when wet. *Rule:* fine particles lift when the surface is dry and the car is fast. *Facts:* road surface, speed, dryness. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* medium.
- **Puddles that stay**: Low spots in lots and driveways hold puddles for days after rain, and dry from the edges inward. *Rule:* water pools where the ground has no slope and dries about 0.1 to 0.25 inch a day in summer, much slower in winter. *Facts:* ground height map, rain total. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Ice in the same spot every winter**: Water from a downspout or sump pump runs across a sidewalk and freezes as a patch. *Rule:* water that flows onto cold concrete freezes below 32 F, and sump pumps keep running when the ground thaws. *Facts:* downspout and sump outlet position, temperature. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Salt stains on sidewalks and steps**: White crusty salt marks collect by doors and on concrete after winter. *Rule:* de-icing salt dries into a pale film, and concrete laid late in the fall scales and flakes from repeated salting. *Facts:* salt applied (a running count) per sidewalk, concrete age. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Scaled and flaking concrete**: Old driveways lose their top layer in rough pitted patches. *Rule:* freeze-thaw with salt flakes the surface, worst on concrete under about 5 years old that was not sealed. *Facts:* concrete age, salt count, freeze-thaw count. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* medium.
- **Heaved sidewalk slabs**: One slab of sidewalk sits an inch higher than its neighbor beside a big tree. *Rule:* roots grow under the slab and lift it, and frost heave adds to it. *Facts:* tree species and trunk size, distance to walk, slab age. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Weeds in the cracks**: Green weeds grow from sidewalk and curb cracks in summer, and dry out in winter. *Rule:* seeds blow into cracks where a little soil and water collect. *Facts:* crack count, property upkeep level, season. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Moss on the north side**: Shaded concrete, roof shingles, and wood fences turn green on the north side. *Rule:* damp, shady spots grow moss and algae; Indiana gets about 40 inches of rain a year. *Facts:* shade, age, direction. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Oil stain in the parking spot**: A dark oily patch sits where an old car always parks. *Rule:* older cars leak a few drops of oil a day on the same spot. *Facts:* car age, parking spot, days parked. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Tire marks at the stop sign**: Black rubber arcs sit at the busiest stop signs and the lot exit by the school. *Rule:* hard braking and fast starts leave rubber on the pavement. *Facts:* intersection use count. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Gum and spill spots around the school and store**: Dark dots cover the sidewalk near schools and shops. *Rule:* gum, drinks, and spills build up where people wait, and rain does not remove them. *Facts:* foot traffic count, age of walk. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Footprints in fresh snow**: Fresh snow shows footprints, and a packed path by the second day. *Rule:* snow compacts to ice under repeated steps. *Facts:* snow depth, wear number. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Snow pile from the plow lasts for weeks**: The pile at the edge of a parking lot turns gray and then black and is still there in March. *Rule:* plowed snow carries dirt, and big piles hold for weeks after the lawn is bare. *Facts:* snow amount plowed, location, dirt amount. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Sledding runs**: A packed ice strip forms down the one hill in town, like a retention pond bank or an overpass slope. *Rule:* kids use any slope they can find in flat country, and runs polish with use. *Facts:* hill slope, snow depth, kids nearby. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Baseball infield dirt and bare base paths**: Little-league diamonds have bare dirt around the bases and a chalk line fading by Thursday. *Rule:* players run the same paths each game, and chalk wears off. *Facts:* games played, last chalking. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Muddy goal mouth on the soccer field**: Fields are bald in front of the goals and along the center line by October. *Rule:* play concentrates there and wet fall weather turns it to mud. *Facts:* games played, rain. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Cattle paths in the pasture**: Parallel narrow trails lead from the gate to the water tank and shade trees. *Rule:* cattle follow the same route, and the soil packs hard. *Facts:* animal count, water position. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Spring thaw reveals the trash**: Cups, bags, and bottles appear along road ditches as the snow melts. *Rule:* litter thrown in winter is covered by snow and shows up in March. *Facts:* road traffic count, litter amount, snow cover. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Spring clean-up crews**: Volunteers and road crews pick litter in April and May, and ditches look clean for a few weeks. *Rule:* many counties hold spring clean-up days around Earth Day. *Facts:* litter amount, date. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Leaf piles at the curb**: In Oct and Nov, rows of raked leaf piles line the street until the city truck vacuums them. *Rule:* towns announce leaf pick-up weeks; kids jump in piles. *Facts:* tree leaf-drop amount per yard, pick-up schedule. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Clogged storm drains**: Leaves block the drains after a windy day and rain leaves a big puddle at the corner. *Rule:* leaves collect against grates, and drains clog most in November. *Facts:* leaf drop, drain position, last cleaning. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Pollen film on everything**: Cars and porch furniture covered in a film of yellow-green pollen in April and May. *Rule:* tree pollen peaks in late April to May in Indiana, and coats everything. *Facts:* date, tree species count, rain. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.

## 2. Weather and seasons

- **Last spring frost**: Tender flowers and gardens are not planted until the frost date passes. *Rule:* average last spring frost in Grant County is late April, with a risk of frost into early May. *Facts:* date, daily low. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **First fall frost**: Gardens blacken and house plants come inside after the first hard frost. *Rule:* average first fall frost is about the middle of October. *Facts:* date, daily low. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Frost on windshields**: Cars have frosted windows on clear, calm, cold mornings and people scrape or warm them up. *Rule:* frost forms when a surface cools below the dew point and 32 F on clear nights. *Facts:* temperature, cloud cover, dew point, parked outdoors. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Cars warming up in the driveway**: Exhaust puffs from parked running cars at 6:30 am in January. *Rule:* many people let a car idle 3 to 5 minutes to clear glass and warm the cabin. *Facts:* person trait, temperature. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Breath and exhaust steam**: Breath, tailpipes, and furnace vents make white clouds below about 25 F. *Rule:* warm moist air condenses in cold air. *Facts:* temperature, humidity. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Frost patterns on old windows**: Single-pane windows frost on the inside in deep cold. *Rule:* moist indoor air meets glass below freezing. *Facts:* window age, outdoor temp. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Icicles under the eaves**: Long icicles hang from rooflines in a thaw after snow. *Rule:* heat leaks through poor attic insulation, melts the snow, and the water refreezes at the cold edge. *Facts:* house age, insulation level, snow on roof, temperature swing. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Snow stays on the well-insulated roof**: One roof is snowy while its neighbor is bare. *Rule:* a heat-leaking roof melts the snow first. *Facts:* house insulation level. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Snow on the grass but not the street**: Streets go bare while lawns stay white after salted plowing. *Rule:* salt and traffic melt snow on roads at 25 F or above, but salt stops working well near 15 F. *Facts:* road salt count, temperature. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Drifts behind the fence and across open roads**: Wind-packed snow drifts across county roads next to open fields and stays out of woods. *Rule:* wind drops snow where something slows it down, like fences, ditches, and hedgerows. *Facts:* wind direction and speed, snow depth, field openness. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Snow fence of corn stalks**: Some farmers leave rows of stalks standing near roads to catch drifts. *Rule:* standing rows trap blowing snow before it reaches the road. *Facts:* field edge, crop left. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Snow days and two-hour delays**: Buses do not run; kids are outside sledding. *Rule:* schools call a closing or delay in the early morning when roads are bad, and e-learning days count as school days in Indiana. *Facts:* road condition, school decision. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Lingering snowmen**: A snowman melts from the south side, and leaves a stick-and-carrot puddle. *Rule:* sunshine melts snow on the south side first. *Facts:* date built, temperature, sun. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Snow that does not pack**: Dry powdery snow in the deep cold will not make snowballs, but wet snow near 32 F will. *Rule:* snow holds together best near the melting point. *Facts:* temperature, snow type. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Black ice at dawn**: Bridges and overpasses ice up before roads. *Rule:* air under a bridge cools it, so it freezes before the ground does. *Facts:* road is bridge, temperature, wetness. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Slow traffic in ice**: Cars slow in ice or snow and distance between them grows. *Rule:* drivers adjust speed to conditions, and braking distance on ice can be ten times longer than on dry pavement. *Facts:* road friction, driver trait. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Spring thaw mud**: Fields and driveways turn to deep mud in March after the frost leaves. *Rule:* frozen ground thaws from the top first, so water can't drain and the top layer is soup. *Facts:* soil frost depth, rain. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **March winds**: Flags snap straight, trash cans roll, and plastic bags fly into fences. *Rule:* March is Indiana's windiest month. *Facts:* wind speed. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Tornado watch and sirens**: Sirens test on a set day, and people look at the sky on warm, humid spring afternoons. *Rule:* peak tornado season in Indiana is March to June; the state holds a statewide tornado drill in March. *Facts:* weather, date. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Storm cell rolling in from the west**: A dark wall with a green tinge approaches and the wind shifts to cold gusts before rain. *Rule:* storms move in from the west or southwest and the outflow wind comes first. *Facts:* storm position and heading, intensity. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* high.
- **Hail dents**: After a hail event, cars and metal roofs carry round dents, and some roofs get a blue tarp. *Rule:* hailstones over about 1 inch dent steel and damage shingles. *Facts:* hail size, building roof age, car parked outside. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Sidewalk rain crowds**: People walk faster in a downpour and few umbrellas appear; hoods go up. *Rule:* people in the Midwest often wear hoods more than carrying umbrellas. *Facts:* rain, personal trait. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Steam off hot pavement after rain**: A summer shower on a hot road makes steam rise. *Rule:* rain evaporates off pavement that is much warmer than the air. *Facts:* road temp, rain. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Heat shimmer over the road**: Distant roads shimmer on a hot, calm day. *Rule:* hot air near the surface bends light. *Facts:* air temp, calm wind. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Humid Indiana summer**: July highs average about 85 F with muggy dew points in the 60s and 70s; the air looks hazy. *Rule:* moisture from the Gulf of Mexico flows north in summer. *Facts:* date, dew point. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Air conditioner drip and hum**: Window and outdoor AC units run on hot afternoons and drip on the ground. *Rule:* AC condenses water from the air. *Facts:* house has AC, outdoor temp. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **People sitting on porches in the evening**: Porches fill after supper in summer and are empty in winter. *Rule:* people cool off outside after dark when it is under about 85 F. *Facts:* personal habit, temperature, time. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Sprinklers running at dawn and dusk**: Lawns are watered early or late and the grass in dry spells is brown by August. *Rule:* watering at midday wastes water and lawn grass goes dormant when dry. *Facts:* rain in the last 14 days, owner habit. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Drought brown in August**: Lawns and roadsides turn tan after about two weeks without rain, then green again within days of a soaking. *Rule:* cool-season grass goes dormant in heat. *Facts:* rain history, lawn irrigation. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Fog in the river valley**: Low fog lies over the river and ditches at dawn in September and October. *Rule:* cool nights near water and warm days make radiation fog that burns off by 9 or 10 am. *Facts:* temp, dew point, calm, location. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Fall color peaks late October**: Maples turn red and orange, tulip poplars yellow, oaks russet, and sycamores brown first. *Rule:* leaf color peaks mid to late October in central Indiana. *Facts:* tree species, date, cold nights. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Early dark in winter**: Sunset comes about 5:20 pm in December, and the days feel short. *Rule:* at 40.5 N, winter days are about 9 hours 20 minutes and summer days about 15 hours. *Facts:* date. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Daylight saving time shift**: After the March clock change, mornings are dark again and school buses run in the dark. *Rule:* Indiana is on Eastern time and observes daylight saving time. *Facts:* date. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Ice storm limbs**: A rare freezing rain leaves a glazed look and branches crack. *Rule:* a half inch of ice adds hundreds of pounds on a limb. *Facts:* ice amount, tree species strength. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Rain barrels and gardens after rain**: Gardeners come out when the soil is dry enough to dig. *Rule:* clay soils are too wet to work for a day or two after rain. *Facts:* rain history, gardener habit. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Rainbows**: Rainbows appear when sun is low and rain is on the other side. *Rule:* needs sun behind the viewer under 42 degrees high. *Facts:* sun angle, rain cell. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* medium.
- **Heat lightning and distant flashes**: Summer nights flash in the distance without thunder. *Rule:* storms too far away to hear. *Facts:* storm position. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.

## 3. Trees and plants

- **Trees that really grow**: A sapling planted this year is a tall shade tree 20 years later. *Rule:* silver maple grows 3 to 5 feet a year, oaks and sugar maples 1 to 2 feet, tulip poplar over 2 feet. *Facts:* species, seed, planting date. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **New houses have little trees**: New streets are bare with staked saplings; old streets have huge trees. *Rule:* trees need decades to fill out. *Facts:* planting date per tree. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Staked saplings with tree wrap**: Young street trees have stakes and straps for the first 1 to 2 years. *Rule:* stakes hold new trees until roots take. *Facts:* planting date. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Bare winter shapes**: Each species has its own winter outline: oak is knobby, maple is rounded, and sycamore has white upper bark. *Rule:* branching habit is set by species. *Facts:* species, seed. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Leaf-out order in spring**: Willows and maples leaf first, oaks and walnuts last, in April and May. *Rule:* species differ in spring timing; black walnut and ash are among the latest. *Facts:* species, warm-day count. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Flowering order**: Silver maple flowers in March, redbud and dogwood in mid to late April, crabapple and lilac in May. *Rule:* each plant has a bloom window tied to the cumulative warmth of spring. *Facts:* species, warm-day count. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Callery (Bradford) pear in white bloom**: Streets turn white in April with a strong smell, and by about 15 to 20 years the trees split in storms. *Rule:* this pear has weak branch angles and short life; Indiana is phasing out sales. *Facts:* species, age, storm count. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Ash trees dying**: Dead gray ash trunks stand in fencerows and yards. *Rule:* emerald ash borer killed most Indiana ash trees starting in the 2000s and 2010s. *Facts:* species ash, year. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Elms are rare and old streets have gaps**: Town streets once lined with elms now have a mix of maples and oaks. *Rule:* Dutch elm disease wiped out most street elms in the 1950s to 1970s. *Facts:* street age, planting history. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Silver maple branch drop**: After storms, limbs of old silver maples lie in yards. *Rule:* their wood is soft and brittle. *Facts:* species, age, wind peak. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Storm-broken limb on a power line**: A limb lands on lines and the lights go out on a street. *Rule:* trees near lines fail in storms. *Facts:* tree distance to line, storm, trimming date. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Tree trimming crews under the power lines**: Trucks with bucket lifts trim trees near lines on a rotation, leaving a V-shaped cut. *Rule:* utilities trim every 3 to 5 years. *Facts:* line position, last trim. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Black walnut nuts in October**: Green-husked walnuts fall on drives, cars roll over them, squirrels bury them, and hands stain brown. *Rule:* nuts fall in early autumn. *Facts:* species, date. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Squirrels bury and forget**: New oak seedlings pop up in odd places. *Rule:* squirrels bury acorns and forget some. *Facts:* squirrel count, oak mast year. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Oak mast years**: Some autumns the ground is covered in acorns, other years almost none. *Rule:* oaks produce heavy crops every 2 to 5 years. *Facts:* year, species. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Sweet gum and sycamore seed balls**: Spiky balls under sweet gums and button balls under sycamores hurt bare feet. *Rule:* seed pods drop in fall and last through winter. *Facts:* species. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Maple helicopters**: Winged seeds spin down in May and clog gutters; little maple seedlings sprout in cracks. *Rule:* silver and red maples drop seeds in spring. *Facts:* species, date. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Cottonwood fluff**: White fluff drifts in early summer near rivers. *Rule:* cottonwood seeds ride cotton tufts in late May to June. *Facts:* species near water, date. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Ginkgo all-at-once drop**: A ginkgo turns gold and sheds all leaves in a day or two after a frost. *Rule:* ginkgo leaves drop together after a hard cold night. *Facts:* species, first frost. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Leaves that hold on**: Oaks and beeches keep brown leaves through winter. *Rule:* some species hold dead leaves until spring growth pushes them off. *Facts:* species, age. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Evergreen windbreak around the farmstead**: Farm homes are ringed by a row or two of pines, spruces, or cedars on the north and west sides. *Rule:* windbreaks cut winter wind and drifting snow. *Facts:* farmstead location, planting date. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Hedgerows of osage orange**: Old fencerows hold thorny trees with bumpy green "hedge apples" in fall. *Rule:* farmers planted it as living fence in the 1800s and early 1900s. *Facts:* fencerow position, species, age. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Eastern red cedars in old fencerows**: Dark cedars with blue berries line field edges where birds dropped seeds. *Rule:* birds sit on fences and seeds fall below. *Facts:* fence position, age. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Tree grows into the fence**: Old wire fences are swallowed by trunks. *Rule:* trees grow around wire over decades. *Facts:* fence age, tree growth. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Roots breaking pipes and lifting walks**: A big tree plus an old house sewer line leads to a backed-up drain. *Rule:* roots seek water in cracked clay pipes. *Facts:* tree size, house age. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Tree rings and stumps**: A cut tree leaves a stump whose rings match its years. *Rule:* one ring per year, with thin rings in drought years. *Facts:* planting date, weather history. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Stump grinding sawdust**: A fresh stump grinding leaves a pile of chips that fades over a year. *Rule:* removal crews grind below grade. *Facts:* removal date. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Firewood piles**: Neatly stacked wood sits against sheds and garages, bigger in autumn, smaller by March. *Rule:* people cut and stack wood in fall and burn it from October to April. *Facts:* house has wood stove, date. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Wood smoke from chimneys**: A gray thread of smoke rises from chimneys on cold evenings. *Rule:* wood stoves and fireplaces burn at night. *Facts:* house has wood heat, temp. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Christmas tree season**: Cut trees appear on porches and curbs from late November, then go to the curb the week after New Year. *Rule:* many trees are taken down in the first week of January. *Facts:* date, household tradition. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Lilacs in May**: Old farmsteads show lilac clumps by the house. *Rule:* lilacs bloom in May and last for 100 years. *Facts:* house age, date. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Peonies by Memorial Day**: Peonies bloom late May around fences and cemeteries. *Rule:* peonies bloom about two weeks around Memorial Day in Indiana. *Facts:* date, planted by old homes. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Daylilies in the ditches**: Orange "ditch lilies" bloom along roads in late June and July. *Rule:* old orange daylilies spread by roots. *Facts:* road ditch, date. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Roadside flower calendar**: Dandelions (April), daisies and clover (June), Queen Anne's lace and chicory (July), goldenrod and ironweed (late summer to fall). *Rule:* each wildflower blooms in its window. *Facts:* ditch plant list, date. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Milkweed and monarchs in late summer**: Monarchs flutter over milkweed in August and September on the way south. *Rule:* monarchs migrate through Indiana in late August to September. *Facts:* date, milkweed, weather. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Cattails and reeds on pond edges**: Cattails grow thick in wet ditches and ponds. *Rule:* they thrive in shallow standing water. *Facts:* water depth, years. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Poison ivy on fences and trunks**: Red and shiny vines climb old trees and fencelines. *Rule:* it grows where birds drop seeds along edges. *Facts:* edge, age. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Invasive honeysuckle thickets**: Dense bushes crowd woods edges and stay green late into autumn. *Rule:* bush honeysuckle leafs out first and drops last. *Facts:* woodlot edge, age. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Planting calendar of gardeners**: Tomatoes go in the ground after the frost date in May; gardens flourish in July and get cleaned out in October. *Rule:* tomatoes are frost-tender. *Facts:* garden trait, date. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.
- **Fruit stand and roadside produce**: Sweet corn and tomatoes sell from truck beds and card tables in July and August. *Rule:* sweet corn is ready around mid-July in Indiana. *Facts:* date, farm trait. *Where:* both. *Cost:* low.
- **Pumpkin patches and corn mazes**: Fields of orange pumpkins, hay rides, and a cut-out corn maze open from late September to Halloween. *Rule:* pumpkins harvest in September and October. *Facts:* date, farm trait. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
- **Mulch fades**: Mulch beds fade from dark brown to gray in a year and get redone each spring. *Rule:* sun bleaches mulch. *Facts:* last mulch date, owner upkeep. *Where:* desktop. *Cost:* low.

## 4. Houses and yards

- **Houses age by decade**: Foursquares and bungalows (about 1900 to 1930), small post-war cottages (1940s-50s), ranches and split-levels (1950s to 70s), two-story builder homes (1990s onward). *Rule:* styles follow building booms. *Facts:* build year. *Where:* both. *Cost:* medium.
