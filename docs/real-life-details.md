# Real-life details for Neighbors (Grant County, Indiana)

**Purpose.** This is a long menu of small, true-to-life details that Neighbors can grow from its own rules, so the owner finds them while playing instead of placing them by hand. Each idea is a few stored facts plus a real-world rule, drawn in more or less detail depending on how far it is from the camera.

Written 2026-10-25, from the owner's request that day: a list "far longer than I would even check by hand," built a piece at a time, with him reporting only what "doesn't look realistic."

## How to read the lines

Each line is one idea:

- **Short name**: what the player notices.
- *Rule:* what causes it in real life. Numbers are rough and from general knowledge of east-central Indiana. Check any number before it becomes a hard rule, and tell the owner when one is a guess.
- *Facts:* the few things the game would store. Everything else is worked out from these, from the date, and from the town's own life.
- *Where:* browser, desktop, or both. "Desktop" means the full-detail close-up version. Browser versions are usually the lighter or numbers-only version.
- *Cost:* low, medium, or high effort and computer load. Low is often a few lines on top of facts the game already has.

Detail by distance applies to every idea: full detail near the camera, a lighter look farther out, and just numbers beyond that. Many ideas share the same facts (the date, the weather, a person's income, a road's age), so building a few shared facts well unlocks dozens of lines below.

Shared facts used all over: the date and time; today's weather and the last 7 days of it; sunrise and sunset for 40.5 degrees north; each person's age, job, income, tastes; each building's age, owner income, and last repair date; each road's age, last repair date, and traffic count; each vehicle's age, owner income, and trip history.

## How this list is ranked (re-ranked 2026-10-25)

The owner asked for this list to be organized by impact on the game, biggest first, and for the small stuff to wait. In his words (2026-10-25): "Your ideas on the real life details list of 713 things needs to be organized by what we have the biggest impact." On the halftime concession-stand idea: "That is a very, very, very low priority on the list." On weather: "Weather and seasons is cool, but it's not the most important either. The game can look amazing even if it's always sunny in the summer. So you need to organize that list by what we have the most dramatic impact on the game and work on those things first."

His standing rule: what the game does and how it looks share one performance budget, so a big cost must buy a big change, and detail goes where a player notices it, never to extreme zoom: "if we are able to cut something in order to zoom all the way in on someone's button on their shirt, I'm not sure what value that has."

**Each idea is scored 1 to 5 on four things:**

- **Seen (S):** how much of the screen it covers and how often a player sees it. Ground, buildings, trees, roads, light, and shadow are always on screen = 5. A once-a-season moment = 1. Sound has no screen at all, so it scores 1 here by design.
- **Alive (A):** how much it changes the feeling that this is a real, living town.
- **Play (P):** whether it affects playing, not just looking.
- **Cheap (C):** 5 = low cost, 1 = high cost. Taken from the line's own Cost tag (low is mostly 4 to 5, medium 3, high 1 to 2) and adjusted by judgement.

**Impact = Seen x 3 + Alive x 3 + Play x 2 + Cheap.** The most it can be is 50.

Ties go to what shows in a summer daytime town, at ordinary play heights (100 to 800 m) and close up (20 to 40 m). In practice that means a tie is broken by Seen, then Alive, then Cheap.

**How the lists are cut.** Top 50, then Tier 2 (next 150), Tier 3 (next 250), and Tier 4 (the rest). The cuts count entries, and a grouped entry counts once. The numbers run straight through all four tiers.

**Groups.** 26 ideas that are really one piece of work are grouped into one entry. A group takes the score of its best part, and every part is listed beneath with its own score. Example: "grass wear" and "desire paths" share one wear number per patch of ground, so they are built together.

**Duplicates.** 10 exact duplicates were merged. The kept entry carries the better of the two scores, and the merged line is kept right beneath it, marked "Merged duplicate", so no wording is lost. All 713 ideas are still here: 703 stand on their own or inside a group, and 10 sit under the idea they duplicate.

**Scores are my judgement, not measurements.** Where one looks wrong, say so and it moves. Cost tags were not re-checked for this pass.

**Section numbers.** "(section N)" at the end of a line points to where the idea sat in the old list (1 ground, 2 weather, 3 trees, 4 houses, 5 roads, 6 traffic, 7 farm machines, 8 people looks, 9 people habits, 10 kids and school, 11 crowds and events, 12 farms, 13 downtown, 14 water, 15 night and sky, 16 wildlife, 17 sound, 18 infrastructure, 19 emergencies, 20 money, 21 small-town specifics, 22 construction, 23 detail by distance).
