# Big local factories

Brawn's pitch #16. Four one-of-a-kind plants turn several local goods into one product. Each is a hub run by its owner (see `scripts/sim/big_factories.gd`), and every load moves on a real truck.

Brawn's rule (2026-10-22, strict): everything in the game is designed against real examples of that thing. This page is the side-by-side comparison for each plant. Gameplay benchmark: Cities: Skylines 2's unique industry buildings and specialized-industry chains.

## Scale

Real plants serve whole regions. A Pike County town in the game is a few thousand people, so each plant is drawn at its real layout and building sizes. Its throughput is scaled to what a small town's farms and companies can truck in. Every load keeps its real size and price: a 1,000-bushel grain semi, an 8,000-gallon tanker, a pot-belly trailer of 170 hogs, and a 40,000 lb reefer.

## Ethanol plant (dry mill)

**Real examples:** Cardinal Ethanol in Union City, IN; POET Biorefining in Alexandria, IN; and the many Iowa and Illinois dry mills.

| Real plant | In the game |
|---|---|
| Truck scale, then a drive-through grain receiving shed over a dump pit | Scale slab and receiving shed on the truck drive |
| A bucket-elevator leg (the tallest thing on site) spouting into corrugated steel bins | 46 m leg, with spouts to 2 or 3 bins |
| Hammer mill and slurry building | White panel building beside the leg |
| A row of 3 or 4 stainless fermenters about 15 to 18 m across, joined by a top catwalk | 3 fermenters, one more per line, with a catwalk |
| Beer well, and distillation columns inside a steel structure frame | Beer well tank, 3 columns in a 34 m frame |
| DDGS dryer building with a thermal-oxidizer stack | Dryer building and a 42 m stack |
| A flat storage shed for distillers grains, open on one side, with a push wall | Pole shed with a push wall and a grain pile |
| A multi-cell mechanical-draft cooling tower | 3-cell tower, one more cell per line |
| White ethanol and denaturant tanks inside an earth containment berm | 2 big tanks and 1 small tank inside a berm |
| A tanker loadout canopy | Canopy with loading arms and a tanker |

**Recipe:** a bushel of corn gives about 2.8 gal of ethanol and 17 lb of distillers grains. So 3 corn loads (3,000 bu) make 1 tanker of ethanol and 1 hopper of distillers grains. The distillers grains go to the meatpacker as feed when the town has one.

## Meatpacking plant (hogs)

**Real example:** Indiana Packers in Delphi, IN, and Midwest hog plants like it.

| Real plant | In the game |
|---|---|
| One long, windowless, white insulated-metal-panel building. The harvest (kill) floor end stands taller | Low fabrication and cooler block, with a taller harvest end at the back |
| Ammonia refrigeration condensers and fans on the roof | Rows of condensers with fan rings |
| A brick office on the street side, and a huge staff lot for 2 shifts | 2-storey brick office and a lot across the front |
| Covered livestock pens and unloading docks at one side of the harvest end | Covered pens with gates, and a hog trailer at the dock |
| Reefer trailers backed into a long row of shipping docks | Dock doors down the long side, with reefers |
| Boiler stack, and a wastewater lagoon behind the plant | Stack and a lagoon inside a berm |

**Recipe:** 1 trailer of 170 market hogs (about 285 lb each, 74% carcass yield) plus half a load of feed for the holding pens makes 1 reefer of boxed pork. Pork goes to the town's stores. Until the town has hog farms, hogs come from neighbor towns or the outside world. The plant's first development step is real Indiana practice: contract growers who finish hogs in barns on nearby farms.

## Furniture maker (hardwood)

**Real examples:** the plants in Jasper and Huntingburg, Dubois County, IN, next door to Pike County. That area is known for wood office and home furniture.

| Real plant | In the game |
|---|---|
| A long brick front building with metal sheds added behind it over the decades | Brick front building, with one metal shed per line |
| An open-sided lumber shed of stickered stacks | Pole shed with stacks and sticker lines |
| A row of insulated dry kilns with big doors | 3 kilns, one more per line |
| A sawdust silo with dust-collector cyclones on a stand, ducted to the mill | Silo, 2 cyclones and a duct run |
| A wood-waste boiler stack | Small stack by the silo |

**Recipe:** 2 log loads (about 9,000 board feet of hardwood) make 1 van of solid-wood furniture once it's sawn, dried, machined and finished. The furniture goes to the town's stores.

## RV plant

**Real examples:** the plants of Elkhart and LaGrange counties, IN, where most US RVs are built (Forest River, Thor, Jayco).

| Real plant | In the game |
|---|---|
| Long, narrow, clear-span metal buildings with overhead doors down the long side. Units roll down the line on their frames | 30 m × up to 130 m buildings with side doors and an end door, one per line |
| A yard of steel frames and chassis | Frames stacked on cribbing |
| Rows of finished travel trailers waiting for toter drivers | Rows of white trailers with graphics, windows and hitches |
| A small office by the road | Brick office and lot |

**Recipe:** half a load of steel (frame and chassis), half a load of wood (framing and plywood) and 1 load of parts (windows, appliances and axles, which local factories supply) make 1 towable RV. RVs leave by toter for the region.

## Growth (hub rules)

- Each plant is one per town and never opens a second site.
- It builds up first (more lines), then buys the land directly behind it, one 25 m bay at a time, up to the industrial lot cap.
- If homes or shops stand on that land, the owner asks through the permit bell. When approved, it buys them out.
- Startup cash builds and runs the plant at first. After that it grows only from what the owner keeps of its profit (Capital, shared by every hub). A company pays out part as dividends.
- Growth takes game years. The plant must run near capacity for six months, and it waits at least two years between expansions, about how long a real plant expansion takes to plan, finance and build. A new line is paid from savings. Land behind it is bought with a down payment and a commercial loan its profit can carry, at what land sells for there, so ground near a growing town costs more.
- If the plant keeps losing money on ground the town has made worth far more to developers, its owner may ask on the bell to sell. If you say yes, it closes and the land opens up. If you say no, it won't ask again for five years.
- The town gets property tax and jobs.
- The player sets its course with grow, steady or hold, and can pre-approve land around it.
