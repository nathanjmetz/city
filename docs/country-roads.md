# Country roads: the rule book

Rules for how country roads, their junctions, shoulders and pole lines are drawn. Each came from something Nathan or a playtest saw, and each is checked by `tests/test_country_roads.gd` (pictures: `tools/road_look_shot.gd`).

| Date | What was seen (his words) | Rule now | Source | Code |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-10-25 | "There's a circle node shape at the intersection where my grid meets the highway." | Where country roads meet (county roads, the state highway, streets with nothing built along them, gravel roads), the junction is shaped: each corner gets a 30-40 ft radius, and the paving fills between them. No round pad anywhere. Two roads meeting end to end just meet. | AASHTO Green Book rural intersections; INDOT Design Manual ch. 46 | `Junction._country`, `RoadView` |
| 2026-10-25 | "The white lines on the sides of the road intersect to a T shape at the intersections." | A side road's edge lines stop where its corner radius starts. The through road's edge line and shoulder stop on the side road's side only, from radius to radius; its centre line and far edge line run on. Where neither road runs through (equal roads crossing), every road stops at its radius. | MUTCD 3B.06 | `Junction` breaks, `RoadMesh._rural` |
| 2026-10-25 | "The gravel goes onto the highway." | Shoulders stop with the edge line, wrap round each corner radius, and never lie under the pavement. | | same |
| 2026-10-25 | "The white lines for an intersection are not where they are supposed to be." | A country stop line stands 4 ft before the edge line of the road met (never on its pavement or shoulder), square to the lane, across the approach lane from the centre line to the edge line. Its sign stands beside it, past the shoulder where the corner flares. The side road's centre line runs on to it. No paint on gravel. | MUTCD 3B.16 (4-30 ft from the traveled way) | `JunctionMarks.country_*`, `JunctionView` |
| 2026-10-25 | "The gravel on the side of the road doesn't look like gravel." | Shoulders are #53 crushed limestone: mid grey, a mix of stone sizes that blends to an even grain before it can shimmer, darker and packed by the pavement, frayed into the grass at a ragged outer edge. Gravel roads and the painted county miles use the same darker stone. | photos of SR 18, SR 26 and county roads | `Surfaces.SHOULDER`, `GRAVEL`, `TerrainView` |
| 2026-10-25 | "The power lines don't make any sense." | One pole line per road: 40 ft wood poles about every 130 ft, past the shoulder, all on one side, and every road running the same way keeps to the same side, so a line crosses a road only where it carries on over a side road. North-south lines ride 1 m lower than east-west ones. Poles keep 15 m back from junctions and off every road. Wires sag about 2% of the span. Single-phase on most roads, three-phase on a crossarm along the highway and avenues. Each house within 100 ft of a pole gets a service drop under a transformer can. Streets drawn as country roads have no separate streetlight poles. | REMC/Duke distribution lines; RUS construction drawings | `PoleLine`, `RoadView._pole_runs`, `_rebuild_drops`, `StreetLights` |
| 2026-10-25 (look check) | The state highway through town "keeps its gravel shoulders and no curb". | Where the town has grown along most of a stretch of the state highway or a county road, that stretch is drawn as a town street: curb and gutter, sidewalks, parking where the shoulders were, a double yellow line. Its junctions with town streets get curb returns but no crosswalks (traffic stops at the highway's edge). | SR 18 and SR 9 through Marion | `RoadView._urban`, `RoadMesh._town` |
| 2026-10-25 (look check) | Roads "stop square in the grass". | A road that ends in the open gets a Type III barricade across each lane. | MUTCD 6F.68, 2C.66 | `RoadView._build_dead_ends` |
| 2026-10-25 (look check) | Painted county roads are "blurry streaks" that fade. | The painted county miles blur only across the road, by the pixel's size across it, so seen low along the road they stay crisp. | | `TerrainView.county_road` |

## Corners cut

- Traffic still decides where cars stop from road types alone. At a crossroads of streets that are drawn as country roads (nothing built near them yet), cars stop where a town street's stop line would be, about 9 m behind the painted country one, until the town reaches them. Moving that into the simulation would make it depend on what is drawn, which every copy of a town must agree on.
- The highway through town gets no streetlights yet (the lamps would go on its utility poles).
- Town streets have no pole lines, so only homes along the highway, county roads and country streets get service drops.
- Trees aren't trimmed back from the wires.
